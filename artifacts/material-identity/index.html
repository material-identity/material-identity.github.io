Material data that systems understand.

Every material has properties. Every industry has standards. Yet material information is still difficult to exchange, interpret and compare automatically.

We believe material data should have the same meaning everywhere it is used.

So suppliers, buyers and software systems can work with the same information — without repeatedly translating or interpreting it.

Industry expertise meets shared definitions.

Material Identity is an open-source, vendor-neutral project. We bring established industry standards, material expertise and technology together to create harmonized, machine-interpretable JSON schemas.

Built on industry standards
Preserve established standards, industry-specific requirements and existing intellectual property.
Harmonized by experts
Align material properties, units, definitions and test methods so systems interpret them consistently.
Designed for automation
Make material data suitable for deterministic validation, comparison and conformity matching.

Existing industry standards keep their identity and ownership.

Ready-to-use schemas for real industries.

Documented, versioned schemas for material declarations. The Global Schema is the most generic, universal schema for any declaration. Material Schemas for Metals, Chemicals and Forestry add narrower definitions.

Metals · EN 10168

Steel inspection documents structured along the five information groups of EN 10168, with reusable components for chemistry, companies and measurements.

The central repository includes validation tools, test fixtures, PDF and HTML rendering, and output in one or two languages with localized numbers and dates.

One foundation. Multiple applications.

Material Identity provides shared definitions. Independent applications use them to exchange material data and perform conformity assessments.

A schema makes deterministic matching possible within its scope; it does not prove the accuracy of a declaration.

Bring your industry standards into Material Identity.

Standards organizations, manufacturers, software providers and industry experts can all help build a common language for material data.

  • Explore the existing schemas, documentation and examples as a starting point for your industry.
  • Propose a schema or share your expertise through GitHub issues.
  • Improve existing definitions, report issues or contribute a translation per language.

All schemas, examples, translations and documentation are open source, under the CC BY 4.0 or Apache-2.0 licence.
